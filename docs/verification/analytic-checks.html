4.3

Groundwater and analytic checks

Describes OGR Slip2D 0.1.235

The groundwater engine and several other tools of OGR Slip2D are checked against results that admit no approximation: a closed-form solution, an identity that has to hold exactly, or a requirement of consistency. For each check this page gives the closed form, the model of the test, what the test requires and the value that the same model gives in version 0.1.235.

None of these checks compares the program with its own earlier output: every one has an exact or independently computable target (Section 4.1.2). Required is the tolerance written in the test; obtained is the value of the quantity that the test asserts, measured by running the model of the test in version 0.1.235. The file of each test is linked in its section, and each can be run on its own (Section 4.1.7).

4.3.1The checks at a glance#

In OGR Slip2D

The checks run in the test suite, and the tests behind this page can be run on their own, from a clone of the repository: python tests/_runner.py seepage_v126 transient_v130 mesh_v125 for the first six rows, and the file named in each section for the others. Several groundwater checks are made twice: on the solver, and through the operations layer ogr_api, whose operations the MCP server publishes as tools (Section 5.3), so that the numbers an agent receives are the verified ones.

Source: tests/ · ogr_fem2d/

4.3.2Confined flow: Darcy's law in one dimension#

In a confined, saturated layer between two edges with prescribed heads and no flow across the other boundaries, the head is linear and the discharge follows the law of Darcy (1856):

H(x)=H0+(H1−H0) xLQ=K H0−H1L hu=γw (H−y)\begin{aligned} H(x) &= H_0 + (H_1 - H_0)\,\dfrac{x}{L} \\ Q &= K\,\dfrac{H_0 - H_1}{L}\,h \\ u &= \gamma_w\,(H - y) \end{aligned}H(x)Qu​=H0​+(H1​−H0​)Lx​=KLH0​−H1​​h=γw​(H−y)​

where

H0, H1H_0,\ H_1H0​, H1​
total heads prescribed at x=0x = 0x=0 and x=Lx = Lx=L (m)
KKK
conductivity of the layer (m/s)
hhh
thickness of the layer, across the flow (m)
QQQ
discharge per unit length of the section (m²/s)
u, γw, yu,\ \gamma_w,\ yu, γw​, y
pore pressure (kPa), unit weight of water (kN/m³) and elevation (m)

A linear head belongs to the space of the linear triangles, so the finite-element solution of this problem is exact up to round-off whatever the mesh; any fault in the assembly, in the boundary conditions or in the computation of the flow shows as a difference.

In OGR Slip2D

The solver imposes the prescribed heads by elimination and computes the discharge through any segment of the model (Section 3.7.3). The second test builds the same box through the operations mesh_generate, seepage_bc_set and groundwater_run, and shows, besides, that the conductivity set by hydraulic_set is the one the solver reads.

4.3.3Layered media: harmonic and arithmetic means#

Two layers of conductivities K1K_1K1​ and K2K_2K2​ give two limiting cases, both consequences of Darcy's law and of continuity. When the flow crosses the layers, in series, the same discharge passes through each of them and the head losses add up, which gives the harmonic mean of the conductivities; when it runs along them, in parallel, every layer sees the same head and the discharges add up, which gives the arithmetic mean:

where

LiL_iLi​
length of layer iii along the flow, with L=∑LiL = \sum L_iL=∑Li​ (m)

The check uses a contrast of 100, which puts the numerical method under stress: almost the whole head loss has to take place in the layer of low conductivity, and the flow has to be conserved across the interface.

In OGR Slip2D

4.3.4Anisotropy: the patch test with a full tensor#

The conductivity of a material is a tensor whose principal values K1K_1K1​ and K2=(K2/K1) K1K_2 = (K_2/K_1)\,K_1K2​=(K2​/K1​)K1​ are rotated by the angle θ1\theta_1θ1​ of K1K_1K1​ from the +x+x+x axis, which gives Kxy=(K1−K2)sin⁡θ1cos⁡θ1K_{xy} = (K_1 - K_2)\sin\theta_1\cos\theta_1Kxy​=(K1​−K2​)sinθ1​cosθ1​ (Eq. (3.7.5)). A linear field of head satisfies the steady equation for any constant tensor, since its gradient is constant:

H=A x+B y+Cv=−K ∇H=−(KxxA+KxyBKxyA+KyyB)\begin{aligned} H &= A\,x + B\,y + C \\ \mathbf{v} &= -\mathbf{K}\,\nabla H = -\begin{pmatrix} K_{xx}A + K_{xy}B \\ K_{xy}A + K_{yy}B \end{pmatrix} \end{aligned}Hv​=Ax+By+C=−K∇H=−(Kxx​A+Kxy​BKxy​A+Kyy​B​)​

If that field is prescribed on the whole boundary, the finite-element solution has to reproduce it at every node, and the Darcy velocity has to be the constant vector of Eq. (4.3.4) in every element. It is the patch test of an element. With Kxy≠0K_{xy} \ne 0Kxy​=0 it exercises the rotation of the tensor and the cross terms of the element matrix, which isotropic cases cannot, and it is the rigorous check of both the rotation and the Galerkin assembly.

Required. The head within 10−9 m at every node, for the five tensors; the velocity of every element within 10−6 of the exact one, relative to its larger component; and, for the isotropic case, the magnitude of the gradient equal to A2+B2\sqrt{A^2 + B^2}A2+B2​ within 10−9. Obtained. Heads within 2.5×10−14 m and velocities within 3.6×10−14, for all five.

In OGR Slip2D

The tensor is built from Saturated permeability Ks, K2 / K1 and K1 angle (deg from +X) of the material, and in the unsaturated zone the whole tensor is multiplied by krk_rkr​, so that the ratio and the direction are kept (Section 3.7.2). The tests also check that the tensor is positive definite and that its trace does not change with the rotation.

4.3.5Transient flow: the response to a step in head#

In a confined, fully saturated aquifer the transient equation reduces to the diffusion equation, with the hydraulic diffusivity D=K/SsD = K/S_sD=K/Ss​, where SsS_sSs​ is the specific storage. For a semi-infinite aquifer at the uniform head H0H_0H0​, whose boundary at x=0x = 0x=0 is raised to H1H_1H1​ at t=0t = 0t=0, the solution is the classical profile of the complementary error function (Carslaw & Jaeger, 1959):

∂H∂t=D ∂2H∂x2H(x,t)=H0+(H1−H0) erfc⁡ ⁣(x2D t)\begin{aligned} \dfrac{\partial H}{\partial t} &= D\,\dfrac{\partial^2 H}{\partial x^2} \\ H(x,t) &= H_0 + (H_1 - H_0)\,\operatorname{erfc}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2\sqrt{D\,t}}\right) \end{aligned}∂t∂H​H(x,t)​=D∂x2∂2H​=H0​+(H1​−H0​)erfc(2Dt​x​)​

The front advances as 2D t2\sqrt{D\,t}2Dt​. The solution holds only while the front is far from the other end of the aquifer, and it is the analytical solution, not the solver, that stops applying beyond that: the test compares only where the semi-infinite assumption holds.

The same file checks that the front advances with time and that a storage ten times larger delays it. The same aquifer is built through the operations layer, with the same bound of 1 % at the same two times, and a third time of 106 s has to reproduce the linear steady profile within 10−3 of the head step.

In OGR Slip2D

The transient solver integrates the mixed form of Richards' equation in time with a modified Picard iteration, by stages (Section 3.7.8). In a saturated zone the storage is the elastic specific storage SsS_sSs​, which is what keeps the system from collapsing onto the steady state.

Source: tests/test_transient_v130.py · tests/test_groundwater_ops_v1200.py

4.3.6Long time: the steady state#

As time goes on the stored water stops changing, and the transient solution has to approach the steady solution of the same boundary conditions, which is that of Eq. (3.7.4):

lim⁡t→∞H(x,t)=H∞(x),∇⋅[ kr(ψ) K ∇H∞ ]=0\lim_{t\to\infty} H(\mathbf{x}, t) = H_\infty(\mathbf{x}), \qquad \nabla\cdot\left[\,k_r(\psi)\,\mathbf{K}\,\nabla H_\infty\,\right] = 0t→∞lim​H(x,t)=H∞​(x),∇⋅[kr​(ψ)K∇H∞​]=0

The unsaturated steady problem has no closed form, so this is a check of consistency and not of a closed form: it ties the transient scheme to the steady solver, which is itself checked against the exact discharge of a rectangular dam (Section 4.3.11).

The permeability function is Gardner's, with Ks=1×10−5K_s = 1\times10^{-5}Ks​=1×10−5 m/s, a=1a = 1a=1 and n=3n = 3n=3; the retention curve is van Genuchten's, with α=0.1\alpha = 0.1α=0.1 m−1 and n=2n = 2n=2, θs=0.4\theta_s = 0.4θs​=0.4 and θr=0.05\theta_r = 0.05θr​=0.05; the specific storage is 1×10−41\times10^{-4}1×10−4 m−1. 4.3.7The finite-element mesh keeps the area#

A conforming mesh of linear triangles that covers a region exactly has to add up to the area of the region:

∑eAe=AΩAe=12∣(x2−x1)(y3−y1)−(x3−x1)(y2−y1)∣\begin{aligned} \sum_e A_e &= A_\Omega \\ A_e &= \tfrac12\left|(x_2 - x_1)(y_3 - y_1) - (x_3 - x_1)(y_2 - y_1)\right| \end{aligned}e∑​Ae​Ae​​=AΩ​=21​∣(x2​−x1​)(y3​−y1​)−(x3​−x1​)(y2​−y1​)∣​

The identity fails if the mesh has gaps, overlaps or triangles that leave the domain, so none of these faults can pass it. It is checked on three models:

  • a square of 10 m by 10 m, with a target element size of 1.5 m: 100 m²;
  • two squares stacked and sharing an edge, which exercise the conformity of the interface between two regions: 200 m²;
  • a slope of the test suite with three material regions, at target numbers of 200, 800 and 2 000 elements: the total area must be the same at every refinement and equal to the area of the regions as the program computes it.

Required. A difference below 10−6 m² in each case, and a spread of the areas below 10−6 m² across the three refinements. Obtained. No difference at floating-point precision in any of them. The same file checks that no edge is shared by more than two elements, that no element crosses the interface between regions and that the nodes of a shared edge are shared, that every element has a positive area and is numbered anticlockwise, that the smallest angle stays above 20° on the square and above 15° on the slope, and that the shape functions reproduce a linear field exactly: the gradient of 3x−2y+73x - 2y + 73x−2y+7 comes out right within 10−6.

In OGR Slip2D

The mesh is built region by region, and the nodes of an edge shared by two regions are shared by both, so the mesh conforms across interfaces by construction (Section 3.7.4).

4.3.8Support back analysis: active and passive forces coincide at a factor of 1#

The back analysis fixes the factor of safety at a target FtF_tFt​ and solves for the horizontal force that has to be added to reach it (Section 3.10.6). With RRR and DDD the resisting and the driving sums of the method, evaluated at FtF_tFt​, and aaa the arm factor of that section,

Tact=1a(D−RFt)Tpas=1a(Ft D−R)=Ft Tact\begin{aligned} T_{\mathrm{act}} &= \dfrac{1}{a}\left(D - \dfrac{R}{F_t}\right) \\ T_{\mathrm{pas}} &= \dfrac{1}{a}\left(F_t\,D - R\right) = F_t\,T_{\mathrm{act}} \end{aligned}Tact​Tpas​​=a1​(D−Ft​R​)=a1​(Ft​D−R)=Ft​Tact​​

so that the two assumptions give the same force at Ft=1F_t = 1Ft​=1, whatever the slope, the surface and the method, and the passive force is the larger one for any target above 1 when a force is needed. The identity would break under almost any algebraic slip, because D−R/1D - R/1D−R/1 and 1⋅D−R1\cdot D - R1⋅D−R are the same expression. The force is zero when FtF_tFt​ is the factor of safety of the surface without support.

4.3.10DXF round trip#

A model exported to DXF and imported again has to come back as the same geometry. Stated precisely, the drawing is not vertex for vertex identical, and it should not be: the importer splits the boundaries at their crossings, so the external boundary gains nodes where a material boundary or a water line meets it. What has to hold is that the shape is unchanged:

  • the enclosed area is the same, with A=12∣∑i(xi yi+1−xi+1 yi)∣A = \tfrac12\left|\sum_i (x_i\,y_{i+1} - x_{i+1}\,y_i)\right|A=21​∣∑i​(xi​yi+1​−xi+1​yi​)∣;
  • every original vertex is still present;
  • every added vertex lies on an original segment.

That is stronger than asking for identical lists, because it still catches a coordinate that is moved, dropped or rounded.

Model. A slope of the test suite whose external boundary has eight vertices, with two material boundaries and a water table from (0, 32) to (120, 22), exported in metres and imported with a welding tolerance of 0.05 % and no simplification; the area is also checked after a round trip in millimetres. Required. The same boundary types and counts; a relative difference of the enclosed area below 10−9; every original vertex present, matched to 10−6; every added vertex within 10−6 m of an original segment; the material boundaries unchanged to nine decimals; the closed or open character of each boundary kept; and the regions still buildable. Obtained. A difference of area of 0, in metres and in millimetres, and an external boundary that goes from 8 to 13 vertices, the five added vertices at up to 4×10−15 m from the original outline.

In OGR Slip2D

The geometry goes to layers that the importer recognises (OGR_EXTERNAL, OGR_MATERIAL, OGR_WATER_TABLE and the others) and the drawings of results go to layers whose name starts with OGR_X_, which the importer ignores, so that re-importing cannot turn a load arrow into a material boundary; a test checks the layer contract and the units recorded in the header (Section 2.2.5).

Source: tests/test_dxf_export_v148.py · ogr_core/dxf/

4.3.11A rectangular dam: the discharge of Dupuit and Charnyi#

For flow through a rectangular dam of width LLL on an impervious base, with an upstream level H1H_1H1​ and a downstream level H2H_2H2​ above the base, the discharge per unit length is, exactly, the following expression (Charnyi's result, which is also the discharge of Dupuit's approximation), provided that the zone above the free surface does not conduct:

q=K (H12−H22)2Lq = \dfrac{K\,(H_1^2 - H_2^2)}{2L}q=2LK(H12​−H22​)​

The unsaturated formulation of OGR Slip2D lets that zone conduct, so the computed discharge is legitimately higher. Making the permeability function sharper has to drive it, monotonically, down to the closed form, and that convergence is the rigorous test: it validates the solver and it explains the excess.

The permeability function is that of Gardner (1958), with a floor of kr=10−8k_r = 10^{-8}kr​=10−8, in four increasingly sharp versions, listed in Table 4.3.6.

4.3.12The retention curve and its derivative#

4.3.13Rapid drawdown: the factor of safety against level and time#

Two properties of rapid drawdown are checked, one against a published result and one as a physical requirement.

The complete drawdown of a homogeneous slope. For a homogeneous slope of the kind charted by Morgenstern (1963), the reference values used by the test suite put the lowest factor of safety at the complete drawdown, 1.20, against 1.41 at half drawdown. The sweep of drawdown levels repeats the whole search at each level (Section 3.8.6). Model. A slope of 3 horizontal to 1 vertical, with c′/γH=0.025c'/\gamma H = 0.025c′/γH=0.025, ϕ′=30∘\phi' = 30^\circϕ′=30∘ and γ/γw=2\gamma/\gamma_w = 2γ/γw​=2, undrained behaviour with Bˉ=1\bar B = 1Bˉ=1, and a full reservoir up to the crest; Bishop's method, a grid of 3 × 4 intervals, 18 slices and four levels from the crest to the toe. Required. The critical level is the complete drawdown; its factor of safety is within 6 % of 1.20; the factor at the intermediate level closest to half drawdown is higher; and sweeping the levels reveals no unsafe margin above 1 %. Obtained. 1.2103 at the complete drawdown, 0.86 % above 1.20, and 1.304, 1.673 and 2.588 at one third, two thirds and the full height of the reservoir.

The factor of safety in time. With the transient analysis, the factor of safety of the stages flagged Calculate SF is lowest right after the level drops, when the pressures have not yet dissipated, and recovers as they do (Section 3.8.7 and Figure 3.8.4). The test builds a drawdown on a slope of the suite with three materials, whose heads fall from 45 m and 30 m on the two sides to 28 m and 26 m, with two flagged stages at 104 and 106 time units, and requires that the factor of safety of the second stage is higher than that of the first. It is a requirement of ordering and not a closed form, and it passes in version 0.1.235.

In OGR Slip2D

The sweep repeats the search at every level because the critical surface moves with the level: a surface held fixed would report a factor of safety that is too high, and a test guards that choice. The numbers of the sweep are the same through the interface and through the operations layer.
