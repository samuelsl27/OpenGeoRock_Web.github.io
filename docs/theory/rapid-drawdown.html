3.8

Rapid drawdown

Describes OGR Slip2D 0.1.235

When the water against an embankment or a natural slope is lowered faster than the soil can drain, a zone of low permeability keeps much of its pore pressure while losing the support of the water outside. Rapid drawdown is therefore one of the governing cases for the upstream slopes of dams and for the banks of reservoirs. OGR Slip2D offers four procedures for this case (an effective-stress analysis with Skempton's Bˉ\bar BBˉ and three multi-stage procedures with undrained strengths), a sweep of the drawdown level and, as an alternative to the assumption of no drainage, a transient seepage analysis.

3.8.1The problem#

Before a drawdown the slope is in equilibrium under the water outside it, which loads its face, and under the pore pressures inside it. A rapid drawdown removes the external water, but the pore pressures in a soil of low permeability cannot follow at once: the soil is unloaded in undrained conditions, and its strength after the drawdown depends on the effective stresses it had been consolidated to before it (Lowe & Karafiath, 1960). A free-draining soil, in contrast, follows the new water level. The procedures in use differ in how they estimate the strength of the undrained zones (Wright & Duncan, 1987; Duncan, Wright & Wong, 1990; USACE, 2003).

OGR Slip2D represents the two reservoir levels with two water surfaces: the water table is the initial level and the drawdown line the final, lower one. Without a drawdown line the drawdown is complete. Every procedure works on copies of the model at the two levels, so the model itself is left unchanged. At the final level the drawdown line takes the place of the water table, so that the pore pressures, the ponded water, the saturated unit weights and the slice boundaries all follow it; piezometric lines are not moved.

In OGR Slip2D

The analysis is switched on in Analysis → Project Settings → Groundwater → Advanced (Rapid Drawdown analysis), with the Drawdown method chosen among the four of Table 3.8.1; b_bar is the default. Every procedure requires a groundwater method of water surfaces (water_table or piezo_line), at least one material marked Undrained Behaviour, and a drawdown line that does not lie above the water table; otherwise the analysis is refused with the reason. The procedure is applied to every method of slices selected, and rapid drawdown excludes the transient groundwater analysis and the excess pore pressure from loading.

Source: ogr_slip2d/rapid_drawdown.py · ogr_core/hydraulic/drawdown_levels.py · ogr_core/project/settings.py

3.8.2The four procedures#

The procedures differ only in the strength they give the materials marked undrained after the drawdown (Table 3.8.1). Freely draining materials keep their effective strength and take the pore pressures of the final level in all four.

Since the drained cap can only lower a strength, on any slip surface the procedure of Duncan, Wright and Wong never gives a higher factor of safety than that of Lowe and Karafiath.

3.8.3Effective stress with B-bar#

The Bˉ\bar BBˉ procedure is a single effective-stress analysis at the final level. The materials marked undrained keep the pore pressure they had at the initial level, changed by the reduction of vertical stress that the removal of the water causes, through Skempton's coefficient:

where

Bˉ\bar BBˉ

Δσv\Delta\sigma_vΔσv​ is the weight of the column of water removed above the slice, limited by the ground surface. Under the face of the slope, where the ground lies between the two levels, only the water that stood on the ground is removed (Figure 3.8.1). Because Δσv\Delta\sigma_vΔσv​ is negative, a larger Bˉ\bar BBˉ gives a lower pore pressure after the drawdown: Bˉ=1\bar B = 1Bˉ=1 follows the unloading completely and Bˉ=0\bar B = 0Bˉ=0 keeps the initial pore pressure unchanged. The result is not allowed below zero, since a negative value would be suction created by the drawdown.

With Bˉ=1\bar B = 1Bˉ=1, a complete drawdown and an initial level above the whole slope, the pore pressure after the drawdown is γw\gamma_wγw​ times the depth below the ground surface, that is ru=γw/γr_u = \gamma_w/\gammaru​=γw​/γ. In OGR Slip2D

Bˉ\bar BBˉ is the B-bar of the material's Rapid Drawdown Parameters (0 to 5). The procedure applies to the materials marked Undrained Behaviour, whatever their Bˉ\bar BBˉ: a value of zero keeps the initial pore pressure. The suction cohesion of those slices is set to zero.

3.8.4Undrained strength envelopes#

The multi-stage procedures need the undrained strength of the soil as a function of its consolidation stress, which is obtained from isotropically consolidated undrained (IC-U) triaxial tests. Those tests can be plotted in two ways (Duncan, Wright & Wong, 1990). The envelope of each undrained material is entered with Define Strength… in its Rapid Drawdown Parameters, as either an R or a Kc=1K_c = 1Kc​=1 envelope, and each procedure converts it to the one it needs with the material's ϕ′\phi'ϕ′. 3.8.5Multi-stage procedures#

Stage 1: before the drawdown#

The slope is analysed in effective stresses at the initial level with the chosen method of slices. Its solution gives, for each slice, the effective normal stress and the shear stress on the base, which are taken as the consolidation stresses of the soil before the drawdown:

Stage 3: the drained cap#

An undrained strength above the drained one can only be sustained by negative pore pressures, which cavitation or partial drainage may prevent. Duncan, Wright and Wong therefore add a third stage, and the Corps of Engineers use a composite envelope, the lower of the R envelope and the drained one. In both procedures OGR Slip2D limits the strength of each undrained slice by the drained strength at the normal stress of the solution after the drawdown, and repeats the analysis:

Capping a slice lowers the factor of safety, which changes the normal forces at which the cap is read; the cap is therefore iterated to a fixed point. The factor of safety of the procedure is the lower of those of stages 2 and 3. Reading the drained strength at the stress after the drawdown, rather than at the consolidation stress σfc′\sigma'_{fc}σfc′​, is the choice with which the program reproduces both procedures in the worked example of USACE (2003, Appendix G).

In OGR Slip2D

The iteration starts with ω=0.5\omega = 0.5ω=0.5, halves it each time the factor of safety reverses its direction of change (but not below 0.05), and stops when two passes differ by less than 10−410^{-4}10−4 or after 20 passes. A surface on which the cap is still cycling is reported at the centre of the cycle (the mean of the last two passes) and marked as not settled. The results of every surface include the factors of safety of the three stages, the number of undrained and capped slices and the passes of the cap. A stage whose method produces no factor of safety makes the surface invalid, with the reason given by that stage.

3.8.6Sweep of drawdown levels#

The complete drawdown is not always the critical one. On a homogeneous slope the factor of safety falls steadily as the reservoir is lowered, and the complete drawdown governs (Morgenstern, 1963). In a zoned dam whose upstream shell drains freely, a complete drawdown leaves the shell with no pore pressure, while a partial one leaves water in it near the toe; the minimum can then fall at an intermediate level.

The drawdown level sweep repeats the whole analysis, search included, at a series of final levels. The search is repeated because the critical surface moves with the level: a surface held fixed can miss the minimum the sweep looks for. By default the levels are eleven, equally spaced from the initial level at the middle of the model down to the lowest point of its external boundary, plus the complete drawdown. At each level the drawdown line is moved rigidly so that its mean elevation is that level, or a horizontal line is created if the model has none.

In OGR Slip2D

Groundwater → Drawdown Level Sweep… is available once a rapid-drawdown analysis is set up (Number of levels 2 to 101, eleven by default; Include total drawdown). It reports the critical level and how much analysing only the complete drawdown would overstate the factor of safety, Ftotal/Fmin−1F_\text{total}/F_\text{min} - 1Ftotal​/Fmin​−1, and plots the factor of safety of each method against the level. Its cost is one full search per level and per method.

Source: ogr_core/statistics/drawdown_sweep.py · ogr_core/hydraulic/drawdown_levels.py

3.8.7Transient seepage as an alternative#

The procedures above assume that the undrained materials do not drain at all during the drawdown, and that the free-draining ones drain completely. When the rate of drawdown and the permeabilities are known, the drawdown can instead be modelled as a transient seepage problem (Section 3.7.8). The reservoir level of each stage is lowered through its boundary conditions, and the stability is computed in effective stresses at every stage flagged Calculate SF, with the pore pressures of that stage (suction included, where the material parameters allow it) and with its own ponded water. The result is the factor of safety as a function of time, whose minimum need not fall at the end of the drawdown.

This route needs no undrained envelope, but its answer depends on the permeabilities and storage parameters of the materials, which are often less certain than their strengths.

In OGR Slip2D

The stages, each with its own reservoir level, are defined in Groundwater → Transient Groundwater…; the factors of safety of every flagged stage are kept with its result and shown in Interpret Groundwater. The materials must be of type fem to take the pore pressures of the stages. A transient analysis cannot be combined with the Rapid Drawdown analysis option.

Source: ogr_slip2d/transient_stability.py
Engineering judgement

The procedures rest on different assumptions and can give quite different factors of safety on the same section, so it is worth comparing more than one. The sweep of drawdown levels shows whether the complete drawdown is the critical case, and the undrained envelope should be checked against the tests it comes from.
