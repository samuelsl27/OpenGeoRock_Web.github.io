3.5

Shear strength models

Describes OGR Slip2D 0.1.235

Every method of slices needs the shear strength available on the base of each slice. OGR Slip2D offers 22 strength models, from Mohr–Coulomb and undrained strength to Hoek–Brown, Barton–Bandis, anisotropic and tabulated envelopes. The chapter first shows how a curved envelope becomes a cohesion and a friction angle on every slice, then describes each model: the expression the program evaluates, its default parameters and the source of the formula.

3.5.1How a strength model enters the analysis#

A strength model is a function τf(σn′)\tau_f(\sigma'_n)τf​(σn′​) that returns the shear strength available on a plane under an effective normal stress σn′\sigma'_nσn′​. It enters the analysis through the mobilised shear force on the base of each slice, Eq. (3.1.2). The methods of slices are written for a base with a cohesion and a friction angle (Eq. (3.2.1)), so a model whose envelope is not a straight line is replaced, on each slice, by its tangent at the stress the base carries:

τf(σn′)≈ci+σn′tan⁡ϕi\tau_f(\sigma'_n) \approx c_i + \sigma'_n \tan\phi_iτf​(σn′​)≈ci​+σn′​tanϕi​
tan⁡ϕi=max⁡ ⁣(0, dτfdσn′∣σˉ),ci=max⁡ ⁣(0, τf(σˉ)−σˉ tan⁡ϕi)\tan\phi_i = \max\!\left(0,\ \left.\dfrac{d\tau_f}{d\sigma'_n}\right|_{\bar\sigma}\right), \qquad c_i = \max\!\left(0,\ \tau_f(\bar\sigma) - \bar\sigma\,\tan\phi_i\right)tanϕi​=max(0, dσn′​dτf​​​σˉ​),ci​=max(0, τf​(σˉ)−σˉtanϕi​)

where

ci, ϕic_i,\ \phi_ici​, ϕi​
instantaneous cohesion (kPa) and friction angle (°) of slice iii
σˉ\bar\sigmaσˉ
effective normal stress at which the envelope is read on that slice, taken as zero when it is negative (kPa)

Figure 3.5.1 shows the construction on the default Hoek–Brown envelope. Where it can, the program uses the derivative the model supplies; otherwise it differentiates the envelope numerically with a centred step of max⁡(10−3 kPa, 10−4σˉ)\max(10^{-3}\,\text{kPa},\ 10^{-4}\bar\sigma)max(10−3kPa, 10−4σˉ), one-sided near zero. For Mohr–Coulomb the construction returns c′c'c′ and ϕ′\phi'ϕ′ themselves, whatever σˉ\bar\sigmaσˉ is.

Where the envelope is read#

The stress σˉ\bar\sigmaσˉ is not known before the method has been solved. The first solve reads every envelope at the estimate

σˉ(0)=max⁡(0, Wcos⁡α−u l)l\bar\sigma^{(0)} = \dfrac{\max\left(0,\ W\cos\alpha - u\,l\right)}{l}σˉ(0)=lmax(0, Wcosα−ul)​

where

WWW
total vertical load on the slice: its weight, the loads on its top, any water standing on it, the vertical load of any support crossing it and, under seismic loading, its vertical seismic force (kN/m)
α, l, u\alpha,\ l,\ uα, l, u
inclination (°) and length (m) of the base, and pore pressure on it (kPa)

A tangent taken anywhere other than at the stress the method finally resolves is not the envelope there: it lies above a concave curve, and on an envelope that passes through the origin a tangent read at zero stress carries no strength at all. When at least one slice of the surface has an envelope whose slope changes with the stress, OGR Slip2D therefore solves again with each such envelope read at the effective normal stress of the method's own solution, and repeats until the two agree:

max⁡i∣σˉi(k+1)−σˉi(k)∣max⁡(1 kPa, ∣σˉi(k+1)∣)<10−6\max_i \dfrac{\left|\bar\sigma_i^{(k+1)} - \bar\sigma_i^{(k)}\right|}{\max\left(1\ \text{kPa},\ \left|\bar\sigma_i^{(k+1)}\right|\right)} < 10^{-6}imax​max(1 kPa, ​σˉi(k+1)​​)​σˉi(k+1)​−σˉi(k)​​​<10−6

Slices whose envelope is a straight line keep the estimate of Eq. (3.5.3), which makes no difference to them. A surface that has not settled after 50 solves is reported as not converged rather than given a factor of safety. How this iteration sits inside each method's own iteration on FFF is described in Section 3.3.7.

In OGR Slip2D

The linearisation is BishopSimplified._local_c_phi, shared by every method; the iteration to the method's own stress is the self_consistent_envelope wrapper (tolerance ENVELOPE_STRESS_TOL = 1e-6, at most ENVELOPE_MAX_PASSES = 50 solves), applied to Bishop, both Janbu methods, Spencer, GLE/Morgenstern–Price, Lowe–Karafiath and the two Corps of Engineers methods. The Ordinary method is not wrapped: its base normal force does not depend on cic_ici​ or ϕi\phi_iϕi​, so the first reading is already the fixed point. A slice whose envelope was read at a negative stress and therefore entered the solver with no strength is listed in the analysis notes.

Source: ogr_slip2d/methods/bishop.py · ogr_slip2d/methods/base.py

Models that read more than the normal stress#

Some models depend on where the base is and how it is oriented. For them the solver passes, with σn′\sigma'_nσn′​, a description of the base: its inclination α\alphaα, the depth of its centre below the ground, the elevation of the top of the material layer it lies in, its distance to the nearest point of the ground surface, the local orientation of the bedding when the material is linked to an anisotropic surface, and the vertical effective stress

σv′=max⁡(0, W+Wwb−u)\sigma'_v = \max\left(0,\ \dfrac{W + W_w}{b} - u\right)σv′​=max(0, bW+Ww​​−u)

where

W, WwW,\ W_wW, Ww​
vertical load of the slice (its weight and the vertical loads applied to it) and weight of any water standing on it, with no seismic coefficient (kN/m)
bbb
slice width (m)

The tables of this chapter say which of these each model reads. A model that needs the description but is evaluated without one (for instance when an envelope is plotted) states below what it falls back on.

Tensile strength and partial factors#

Two further properties of a model are used elsewhere. Its tensile strength is the tension the Tensile Stress Check tolerates on a slice base (Section 3.3.6); it is zero for every model except the two Hoek–Brown models, for which it is given in Eq. (3.5.19). Its partial-factor category says what a design standard divides when it is active (Section 3.12): c′c'c′ and tan⁡ϕ′\tan\phi'tanϕ′, the undrained strength cuc_ucu​, or the shear strength τ\tauτ as a whole. 3.5.3Soil models#

Mohr–Coulomb#

The linear envelope of Coulomb (1776), written in effective stresses:

τf=c′+max⁡(0, σn′)tan⁡ϕ′\tau_f = c' + \max(0,\ \sigma'_n)\tan\phi'τf​=c′+max(0, σn′​)tanϕ′

A tensile normal stress contributes no friction: below σn′=0\sigma'_n = 0σn′​=0 the strength stays at c′c'c′. Strength gained from matric suction above the water table is added to c′c'c′ as described in Section 3.5.8.

Undrained strength (φ = 0)#

The total-stress analysis of a saturated clay loaded faster than it can drain, in which the shear strength is the undrained strength and does not depend on the normal stress (Skempton, 1948):

τf=cu\tau_f = c_uτf​=cu​

The single parameter is cohesion = cuc_ucu​ (default 50 kPa).

Undrained strength varying with depth#

In a normally consolidated clay the undrained strength is proportional to the vertical effective stress (Ladd & Foott, 1974), so under level ground it grows linearly with depth, and published soft-ground profiles are stated that way (Duncan & Wright, 2005). Three models implement the straight line

where

The three models differ only in where zzz is measured from (Table 3.5.2). The line is not bounded below zero: where it reaches zero inside a material, that part of the material has no strength, and the analysis notes give the elevation at which this happens.

Drained–undrained#

A drained Mohr–Coulomb envelope capped by a constant strength, equal to the drained strength at a threshold normal stress σt\sigma_tσt​:

τf=min⁡(c′+max⁡(0, σn′)tan⁡ϕ′,  c′+σttan⁡ϕ′)\tau_f = \min\left(c' + \max(0,\ \sigma'_n)\tan\phi',\ \ c' + \sigma_t\tan\phi'\right)τf​=min(c′+max(0, σn′​)tanϕ′,  c′+σt​tanϕ′)

Its parameters are cohesion = c′c'c′ (5 kPa), phi = ϕ′\phi'ϕ′ (28°) and sigma_threshold = σt\sigma_tσt​ (100 kPa). The threshold is a stress, not a strength, and a design standard does not factor it.

The undrained strength normalised by the vertical effective stress and the overconsolidation ratio, after Ladd & Foott (1974):

A strength proportional to the vertical effective stress at the base, with a floor:

3.5.4Rock models#

Hoek–Brown and Generalized Hoek–Brown#

The program's own source records (defect D171) that the envelope of Eq. (3.5.18) "is not yet this criterion's Mohr envelope", and the caveat applies to both Hoek–Brown models. 3.5.5Anisotropic models#

In a bedded or foliated material the strength depends on the orientation of the slip surface relative to the layering. The four anisotropic models read the inclination α\alphaα of each slice base, measured counter-clockwise from the horizontal; the first two compare it with a bedding direction, the last two use it directly.

Anisotropic linear#

A Mohr–Coulomb material whose cohesion and friction change linearly from a bedding strength (c1, ϕ1)(c_1,\ \phi_1)(c1​, ϕ1​) to a rock-mass strength (c2, ϕ2)(c_2,\ \phi_2)(c2​, ϕ2​) as the base turns away from the bedding (Mercer, 2012). With δ\deltaδ the acute angle between the base and the bedding,

Snowden modified anisotropic linear#

The anisotropic linear model with two extensions (Mercer, 2012). The transition may differ on either side of the bedding, and the bedding and rock-mass strengths are functions of the normal stress, each either a shear/normal function or a C/Phi function (Section 3.5.6):

Anisotropic strength function#

A table of ranges of the base inclination itself, each with a constant cohesion and friction angle. With rows (αk, ck, ϕk)(\alpha_k,\ c_k,\ \phi_k)(αk​, ck​, ϕk​) in increasing αk\alpha_kαk​ and the last one at 90∘90^\circ90∘,

A table saved by a version before 0.1.218, when the same numbers were interpolated points, is kept in the file but refused by the analysis until it has been reviewed.

Generalized anisotropic#

Any strength model, or the strength of another material, assigned to a range of base inclination. The ranges are absolute inclinations folded into (−90∘, 90∘](-90^\circ,\ 90^\circ](−90∘, 90∘]; the first rule whose range holds the inclination supplies the strength, a limit belongs to the lower range, and the chosen model receives the same description of the base, so a child model that reads σv′\sigma'_vσv′​ or a depth gets it. Weight, water and every other property are those of the parent material. A set of rules that leaves an inclination uncovered, or whose model cannot be built, is refused before the analysis starts. Without a slice the model returns the weakest of its rules' models.

In OGR Slip2D

A material is linked to an anisotropic surface in Properties → Define Materials…; only the anisotropic linear and Snowden models read the local bedding from it, and a Generalized Anisotropic material that still links one is refused, because its ranges are absolute inclinations. The tangent of ϕ\phiϕ in Eq. (3.5.25) has been interpolated since version 0.1.225; earlier versions interpolated the angle.

3.5.6Tabulated functions#

Three models take the envelope from a table, typically fitted to laboratory results. They involve no formula of their own beyond the interpolation.

For the C/Phi function the program supplies the exact derivative of τf\tau_fτf​, including the change of ϕ\phiϕ with stress, to the linearisation of Eq. (3.5.2); the other two are differentiated segment by segment. A design standard divides the τ\tauτ of every point of the first two tables, and the interpolated ccc and tan⁡ϕ\tan\phitanϕ of the third.

3.5.7Infinite strength and no strength#

infinite_strength models a material that the slip surface cannot shear, such as sound bedrock or a structure; in the linearisation of Eq. (3.5.2) it becomes a cohesion of 101210^{12}1012 kPa with ϕi=0\phi_i = 0ϕi​=0, and a design standard does not factor it. Supports whose capacity depends on the strength of the surrounding ground receive none from it, and the analysis notes say so (Section 3.10). no_strength returns τf=0\tau_f = 0τf​=0, for water or open voids.

3.5.8Unsaturated strength#

Above the water table a seepage analysis returns negative pore pressures, and matric suction adds to the strength of the soil. Fredlund, Morgenstern & Widger (1978) extended Mohr–Coulomb with a second friction angle ϕb\phi^bϕb for the suction:

τf=c′+(σn−ua)tan⁡ϕ′+(ua−uw)tan⁡ϕb\tau_f = c' + (\sigma_n - u_a)\tan\phi' + (u_a - u_w)\tan\phi^bτf​=c′+(σn​−ua​)tanϕ′+(ua​−uw​)tanϕb

OGR Slip2D applies it as a bilinear envelope through two properties of the material, ϕb\phi^bϕb and the air-entry value ψae\psi_{ae}ψae​. With the suction s=−us = -us=−u: when s≤ψaes \le \psi_{ae}s≤ψae​ the negative pore pressure is kept and acts through tan⁡ϕ′\tan\phi'tanϕ′; when s>ψaes > \psi_{ae}s>ψae​ the pore pressure is taken as −ψae-\psi_{ae}−ψae​ and the excess suction adds the cohesion

cs=(s−ψae)tan⁡ϕbc_s = \left(s - \psi_{ae}\right)\tan\phi^bcs​=(s−ψae​)tanϕb

to cic_ici​ of Eq. (3.5.2), for every strength model. Both properties default to zero, which clips negative pore pressures to zero so that suction adds nothing. The suction can also be capped by a negative pore-pressure cutoff. The pore pressures themselves are the subject of Section 3.6.

In OGR Slip2D

The treatment is apply_unsaturated_policy in the slicer, which stores csc_scs​ on each slice so that every method receives it through the same linearisation. A design standard divides tan⁡ϕb\tan\phi^btanϕb by the factor on tan⁡ϕ′\tan\phi'tanϕ′.

Source: ogr_slip2d/slicer.py · ogr_core/materials/material.py
Engineering judgement

An error in the strength model or its parameters passes straight into the factor of safety. Take the envelope and its parameters from tests on the materials of the site, and check that the strength each material receives over the range of stresses of your slope is the one you intended. The default parameters are placeholders and must be replaced before a result is used for design.
