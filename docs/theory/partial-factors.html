3.12

Partial factors (Eurocode 7)

Describes OGR Slip2D 0.1.235

Eurocode 7 verifies a slope by applying partial factors to the actions and to the strength of the ground instead of asking for a global factor of safety. OGR Slip2D can apply the factors of the design approaches of EN 1997-1 to a copy of the model before the analysis. This chapter lists the factors of each preset, explains how each one is applied and what the reported number then means, and states plainly what this version does not apply.

3.12.1Limit-state design#

In limit-state design the stability of a slope is a geotechnical (GEO) ultimate limit state: the design effect of the actions must not exceed the design resistance (CEN, 2004; Frank et al., 2004),

Ed≤RdE_d \le R_dEd​≤Rd​

and the design values come from characteristic or representative values through partial factors, material properties being divided by theirs and actions multiplied by theirs:

Xd=XkγM,Fd=γF FrepX_d = \dfrac{X_k}{\gamma_M}, \qquad F_d = \gamma_F\,F_{rep}Xd​=γM​Xk​​,Fd​=γF​Frep​

where

Xk, γMX_k,\ \gamma_MXk​, γM​
characteristic value of a material property (c′c'c′, tan⁡ϕ′\tan\phi'tanϕ′, cuc_ucu​…) and its partial factor
Frep, γFF_{rep},\ \gamma_FFrep​, γF​
representative value of an action and its partial factor, γG\gamma_GγG​ for permanent and γQ\gamma_QγQ​ for variable actions

EN 1997-1 groups the factors in sets — A1 and A2 on actions, M1 and M2 on materials, R1, R2 and R3 on resistances — and combines them in three design approaches. A limit-equilibrium analysis carried out with design values measures how far the design resistance exceeds the design effect of the actions; Frank et al. (2004) call that ratio the over-design factor, and the limit state is satisfied when it is at least one:

3.12.2Design approaches and presets#

Table 3.12.1 gives the factors of the four Eurocode 7 presets exactly as the program stores them.

The sets behind the presets are those of EN 1997-1, Annex A: DA1-C1 combines A1, M1 and R1; DA1-C2 combines A2, M2 and R1; DA2 combines A1, M1 and R2, whose γR;e=1.1\gamma_{R;e} = 1.1γR;e​=1.1 is the factor on the earth resistance of a slope; DA3 combines M2 and R3 with A2 on geotechnical actions — the actions on a slope are geotechnical — and A1 on structural ones. Two columns are decisions of the program: γcu=1.4\gamma_{cu} = 1.4γcu​=1.4 is the M2 factor on undrained strength, and γτ\gamma_\tauγτ​ is set equal to the γtan⁡ϕ′\gamma_{\tan\phi'}γtanϕ′​ of the same set, so that an envelope that cannot be split into c′c'c′ and tan⁡ϕ′\tan\phi'tanϕ′ receives the same strength reduction. Besides the four presets, a standard can be Custom, with the eight factors entered by hand, or None.

3.12.3How the factors are applied#

The factors are applied to a copy of the model before any analysis runs, so the solver remains a plain limit-equilibrium engine and the user's model is never changed. 3.12.4What the reported number means#

With a design standard on, the partial factors have already been applied to the inputs, so the number the analysis reports is not a factor of safety: it is the over-design factor of Eq. (3.12.3), and the design check requires it to be at least 1. The interface, the command line and the API label it as an over-design factor and report which factors were applied. For a slope whose materials are all of the c′c'c′–ϕ′\phi'ϕ′ kind and which carries no external loads, the over-design factor is F/1.25F/1.25F/1.25 under DA1-C2 and DA3, F/1.1F/1.1F/1.1 under DA2, and FFF itself under DA1-C1, whose material factors are all 1.0.

A standard switched on with every factor left at 1.0 changes nothing, and the analysis says so, because the label would otherwise suggest a factored result.

3.12.5Limitations of this version#

Not applied in this version (D226)

The program's source and its factor report state these limitations; they are reported, not corrected.

  • The permanent-action factor γG\gamma_GγG​ is not applied. The weight of the soil and any permanent load carry no action factor, and every distributed and line load takes the variable-action factor γQ\gamma_QγQ​. Under DA1-C1, whose material factors are all 1.0, a slope with no external loads therefore returns exactly its unfactored factor of safety, and under DA2 it returns F/γR;e=F/1.1F/\gamma_{R;e} = F/1.1F/γR;e​=F/1.1. Every run with γG≠1\gamma_G \ne 1γG​=1 adds a note saying so.
  • DA3 carries the action factors of set A1 (1.35 and 1.5) where a slope, whose actions are geotechnical, takes those of A2 (1.0 and 1.3). As γG\gamma_GγG​ is not applied in any case, the practical effect is that external loads are multiplied by 1.5 instead of 1.3.
  • Every load is treated as unfavourable: γQ\gamma_QγQ​ multiplies all distributed and line loads, including any whose effect is stabilising.
  • Not factored: support capacities, pore pressures, ponded water, the water in a tension crack and the seismic coefficients.

Read the over-design factor of a run with these points in mind, in particular under DA1-C1, DA2 and DA3, where the γG=1.35\gamma_G = 1.35γG​=1.35 of set A1 is not part of the number.

3.12.6Partial factors in the other analyses#

Every analysis reads the factored copy: the search for the critical surface, the transient and rapid-drawdown stages and the drawdown sweep, and the back analysis of support force, which then returns the force needed to reach the target over-design factor (Section 3.10.6). A statistical run samples the model and factors each sample after drawing it, so that a sampled cohesion is factored like the deterministic one; its probability of failure is then the probability that the over-design factor falls below one (Section 3.11). With Compute Ky on, the reported number is a seismic coefficient of the factored model, and the interface and the API label it as one (Section 3.9.2).
