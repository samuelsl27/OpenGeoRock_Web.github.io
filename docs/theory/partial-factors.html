3.12

Partial factors (Eurocode 7)

Describes OGR Slip2D 0.1.481

Eurocode 7 verifies a slope by applying partial factors to the actions and to the strength of the ground instead of asking for a global factor of safety. OGR Slip2D can apply the factors of the design approaches of EN 1997-1 to a copy of the model before the analysis. The number it then reports is an over-design factor, and what no factor reaches is listed under What is not factored.

3.12.1Limit-state design#

In limit-state design the stability of a slope is a geotechnical (GEO) ultimate limit state: the design effect of the actions must not exceed the design resistance (CEN, 2004; Frank et al., 2004),

Ed≤RdE_d \le R_dEd​≤Rd​

and the design values come from characteristic or representative values through partial factors, material properties being divided by theirs and actions multiplied by theirs:

Xd=XkγM,Fd=γF FrepX_d = \dfrac{X_k}{\gamma_M}, \qquad F_d = \gamma_F\,F_{rep}Xd​=γM​Xk​​,Fd​=γF​Frep​

where

Xk, γMX_k,\ \gamma_MXk​, γM​
characteristic value of a material property (c′c'c′, tan⁡ϕ′\tan\phi'tanϕ′, cuc_ucu​…) and its partial factor
Frep, γFF_{rep},\ \gamma_FFrep​, γF​
representative value of an action and its partial factor, γG\gamma_GγG​ for permanent and γQ\gamma_QγQ​ for variable actions

EN 1997-1 groups the factors in sets (A1 and A2 on actions, M1 and M2 on materials, R1, R2 and R3 on resistances) and combines them in three design approaches. A limit-equilibrium analysis carried out with design values measures how far the design resistance exceeds the design effect of the actions; Frank et al. (2004) call that ratio the over-design factor, and the limit state is satisfied when it is at least one:

ODF=RdEd≥1\text{ODF} = \dfrac{R_d}{E_d} \ge 1ODF=Ed​Rd​​≥1

3.12.2Design approaches and presets#

Table 3.12.1 gives the factors of the four Eurocode 7 presets as the program stores them.

The sets behind the presets are those of EN 1997-1, Annex A: DA1-C1 combines A1, M1 and R1; DA1-C2 combines A2, M2 and R1; DA2 combines A1, M1 and R2, whose γR;e=1.1\gamma_{R;e} = 1.1γR;e​=1.1 is the factor on the earth resistance of a slope; DA3 combines M2 and R3 with A2 on geotechnical actions and A1 on structural ones; the actions on a slope are geotechnical, so the program loads A2 for DA3, and on a slope DA3 has the factors of DA1-C2 (Frank et al., 2004). In both sets of actions the favourable permanent factor is 1.0 and the favourable variable factor 0: a variable load that would help may not be there. Two columns are decisions of the program: γcu=1.4\gamma_{cu} = 1.4γcu​=1.4 is the M2 factor on undrained strength, and γτ\gamma_\tauγτ​ is set equal to the γtan⁡ϕ′\gamma_{\tan\phi'}γtanϕ′​ of the same set, so that an envelope that cannot be split into c′c'c′ and tan⁡ϕ′\tan\phi'tanϕ′ receives the same strength reduction. Besides the four presets, a standard can be Custom standard, with the eleven factors entered by hand, or No standard, with all of them at 1.0.

3.12.3How the factors are applied#

The material factors and the seismic factor are applied to a copy of the model before any analysis runs, so the user's model is never changed. The action factors travel with that copy and are applied by the slicer, because whether a weight or a load is favourable depends on the surface. Material factors divide and action factors multiply. The factored weight is the one every term reads: the pseudo-static force, the vertical stress of the strength models that use it, and the pore pressures computed from the weight (rur_uru​ and the excess of the B-bar method). Each strength model declares which category its parameters belong to (Table 3.12.2); a model that declares none is left as it is, and the analysis says so.

Sarma's method also has a strength on the faces between its wedges (Section 3.2). A face whose strength comes from the materials it crosses reads the factored materials of the copy. A face strength given by hand, for every face or at a vertex of a user-defined surface, is a cohesion and a friction angle, and it is divided as a Mohr–Coulomb material is, by Eq. (3.12.4). In OGR Slip2D

Partial factors are set on the Design Standard page of Analysis → Project Settings: Apply partial factors (off by default, because switching it on changes every result previously compared against), the Design standard, the eleven factors, which are editable when the standard is Custom standard, and the options Single source (on by default) and Anchors (off by default), which decide how γG\gamma_GγG​ reaches the slices and the bond of a support (Section 2.11). The analysis reports what was factored, material by material, with the notes of the factor report.

3.12.4What the reported number means#

With a design standard on, the partial factors have already been applied to the inputs, so the number the analysis reports is not a factor of safety: it is the over-design factor of Eq. (3.12.3), and the design check requires it to be at least 1. The interface, the command line and the API label it as an over-design factor and report which factors were applied. For a slope whose materials are all of the c′c'c′–ϕ′\phi'ϕ′ kind and which carries no external loads, the over-design factor is F/1.25F/1.25F/1.25 under DA1-C2 and DA3, whose γG\gamma_GγG​ is 1.0. Under DA1-C1 and DA2 the soil weight takes γG=1.35\gamma_G = 1.35γG​=1.35, which raises the driving side and the frictional resistance alike but not the cohesion, so the result does not follow from FFF alone. On a dry slope with c′=0c' = 0c′=0 it is FFF under DA1-C1 and F/1.1F/1.1F/1.1 under DA2; on an undrained slope, F/1.35F/1.35F/1.35 and F/(1.35×1.1)=F/1.485F/(1.35 \times 1.1) = F/1.485F/(1.35×1.1)=F/1.485 (Frank et al., 2004). On the two-layer slope of Example 1, with a water table and Bishop's method, OGR Slip2D 0.1.481 gives F=1.300F = 1.300F=1.300 and over-design factors of 1.298 under DA1-C1, 1.040 under DA1-C2 and DA3, and 1.180 under DA2.

A standard switched on with every factor left at 1.0 changes nothing, and the analysis says so, because the label would otherwise suggest a factored result.

3.12.5What is not factored#

Version 0.1.481

No partial factor reaches these inputs, whatever the standard:

  • Water outside the soil: the weight and thrust of ponded water and the thrust of the water in a tension crack, which the slicer keeps apart from the soil weight.
  • Pore pressures that do not read the weight: those of a water table, a piezometric line, a grid or a seepage analysis. Only rur_uru​ and the B-bar excess follow the factored weight.
  • The initial state of a B-bar rapid drawdown: the pore pressure before the drawdown of an undrained material whose water is rur_uru​ is read from the unfactored weight, a limitation the program records as not corrected since version 0.1.244.
  • Supports: their forces and capacities are used as entered. With Anchors on, γG\gamma_GγG​ multiplies only the effective vertical stress that a bond reads.

3.12.6Partial factors in the other analyses#

Every analysis reads the factored copy: the search for the critical surface, the transient and rapid-drawdown stages and the drawdown sweep, and the back analysis of support force, which then returns the force needed to reach the target over-design factor (Section 3.10.6). A statistical run samples the model and factors each sample after drawing it, so that a sampled cohesion is factored like the deterministic one; its probability of failure is then the probability that the over-design factor falls below one (Section 3.11). With Compute Ky on, the reported number is a seismic coefficient of the factored model, the seismic factor is not applied, and the interface and the API label it as one (Section 3.9.2). The critical surface of such a run is the one with the lowest coefficient, and its over-design factor need not be the lowest, so the analysis notes of the window and, since version 0.1.449, the PDF report say that the coefficient and the Newmark displacement are computed with the partial factors applied and that the factor shown for the critical surface is an over-design factor.
