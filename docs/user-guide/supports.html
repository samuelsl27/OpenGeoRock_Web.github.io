2.6

Supports

Describes OGR Slip2D 0.1.235

Anchors, soil nails, piles, geosynthetics and walls add a force where a slip surface crosses them. In OGR Slip2D a support is a line from its head, at the slope face, to its tail, inside the ground, and it takes its properties from a named support type defined once for the project. This page explains how to define support types, place supports one by one or as a pattern, edit them, choose between active and passive forces, and find the support force a design needs with the back analysis. How each type develops its force is the subject of Section 3.10.

2.6.1Defining support types#

Supports cannot be placed until the project has at least one support type. Choose Properties → Define Support… to open Define Support Properties. The list on the left, Support Types, holds the named property sets of the project, each shown with its kind in brackets; + Add creates one, Duplicate copies the selected one and − Remove deletes it (the last one cannot be removed). Opened on a project without types, the dialog starts with Type 1, an End Anchored support.

Parameters are labelled with their stored names in words (Out Of Plane Spacing, Bond Length Percent…) and carry their units: kN, kN/m, kPa, m, degrees or per cent. These units are fixed — they are not converted to the unit system of the project. The capacities of anchors, tiebacks, nails and piles are per support, and the program divides them by the out-of-plane spacing to obtain a force per metre of slope; a geosynthetic is described per metre of sheet width, with its strip coverage.

The tieback, nail, geosynthetic and anchor capacities combine several failure modes — tensile rupture, pullout behind the slip surface, stripping of the head or plate — and the force at a crossing is the smallest that applies there. The Ito & Matsui mode of the pile derives its force from the soil flowing between the piles of a row (Ito & Matsui, 1975). The equations are in Section 3.10.

OK stores the list. Supports already placed follow their property set: a change of kind or of parameters applies to them at once. A support whose set has been removed falls back to the first set of the same kind — or, when there is none, to the default properties of that kind — and the analysis notes say so.

2.6.2Force application and orientation#

The force of a support can enter the equilibrium of the sliding mass in two ways (Duncan & Wright, 2005):

  • Active: the force reduces the driving action, as a prestressed anchor does before any movement: F=R/(D−T)F = R / (D - T)F=R/(D−T).
  • Passive: the force adds to the resistance and is mobilised only as the mass moves, as an untensioned nail: F=(R+T)/DF = (R + T) / DF=(R+T)/D, so the support force is divided by the factor of safety like the strength of the soil.

Here RRR and DDD are the resisting and driving sums and TTT the support force. For the same force the two give different factors of safety; the defaults of each kind are listed in Table 2.6.2.

Where application and orientation are stored

Each placed support keeps its own force application and orientation. Add Support copies them from the support type when the support is placed; Add Support Pattern… takes them from its own dialog, where they start at Active and Tangent To Slip whatever the type says. Changing them later in Define Support does not change the supports already placed: edit those with Properties… in their right-click menu, or with Modify Pattern… for a row.

2.6.3Placing supports#

One support at a time#

  1. Choose Support → Add Support.
  2. Click the head, at the slope face, then the tail, the end inside the ground. Clicks are snapped like any drawing.
  3. Repeat for the next support; press Esc to finish.

The order of the clicks matters: the stripping length is measured from the head and the pullout length from the tail, and a force parallel to the support pulls from head to tail. Add Support always uses the first support type of the list in Define Support; to give a support another type, use Support → Modify Support… or Properties… in its right-click menu.

A pattern of supports#

Support → Add Support Pattern… places a row of identical supports along the slope. Fill in the dialog, press OK, then click two points along the slope face: the first support has its head on the first point and the others follow at the in-plane spacing along the segment, as many as fit.

The default angle of −15° points down and to the right, into a slope whose crest is on the right (failure from right to left); for a slope that fails from left to right use an angle such as −165°. With Perpendicular to boundary, click the two points from left to right so that the supports point into the ground.

2.6.4Editing supports#

Changes to supports are not on the undo stack. Supports are not moved when the geometry is edited, so check them after reshaping the slope. Tools → Property Tables → Support Properties Table lists the supports of the model; in the Interpret window, Data → Support Force Analysis… and Data → Support Force Diagram… show the force each support develops along its length and which failure mode governs (Section 2.8).

2.6.5Back analysis of the support force#

Support → Back Analysis of Support Force… answers the design question the other way round: what horizontal force is needed for every slip surface to reach a target factor of safety? The factor is fixed at the target, so the force comes out of the resisting and driving sums without iteration, under both the active and the passive assumptions; the surface that needs the largest force is reported (Duncan & Wright, 2005).

The search configured in Surface Options is run on the model, with the design standard if one is on. The status bar reports the required force for the target factor of safety, the active and passive forces of the governing surface, its factor of safety without support and the number of surfaces analysed. The passive value is the larger of the two, so it is the conservative one. In the Interpret window, Data → Back Analysis… computes the same quantities for the critical surface shown there. The back analysis neither uses nor changes the results of Analysis → Compute.

In OGR Slip2D

Each kind of support is a plug-in that declares its parameters, its default application and orientation and the force it can develop at a distance from its head; the dialog is built from those declarations. Every placed support names its property set, so two sets of the same kind with different capacities stay distinct in the analysis.
