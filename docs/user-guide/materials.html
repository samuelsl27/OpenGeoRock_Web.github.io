2.3

Materials

Describes OGR Slip2D 0.1.235

Every region of the model is made of a material, and a material says three things: how much it weighs, how strong it is and where its pore pressure comes from. Materials are defined in one dialog and then painted onto the regions of the model. This page describes the dialog field by field, the GSI calculator for the Hoek–Brown criterion, how materials are assigned — including what happens to a region nobody assigned — and the tools that list materials or bring them in from another project.

2.3.1Defining materials#

Open Properties → Define Materials… (also on the toolbar, and in the right-click menu of a region). The list on the left holds the materials of the project, each in its colour; + Add creates one and − Remove deletes the selected one. A new material is called Material n and starts as Mohr–Coulomb with c′=10c' = 10c′=10 kPa and ϕ′=25∘\phi' = 25^\circϕ′=25∘.

The editor on the right shows the selected material in up to five groups: General, Strength Type and Water Parameters always, Rapid Drawdown Parameters and Excess Pore Pressure only when the project runs those analyses. Edits are kept while you move from one material to another; OK stores the whole list and Cancel discards every change made since the dialog was opened. There is no Apply button.

General#

The saturated unit weight is the bulk unit weight of the saturated soil, not the submerged (buoyant) one, so it should be greater than the unit weight above the water table; the dialog shows a warning in red when it is not, and keeps the value as typed. The two unsaturated parameters follow the extended Mohr–Coulomb envelope of Fredlund, Morgenstern & Widger (1978); they only matter where a seepage analysis gives negative pore pressures (Section 3.6).

Strength type#

The drop-down list of the Strength Type group offers 22 strength models. The equation of the chosen model is shown beside the list, and its parameters appear below it, each labelled with the name under which it is stored and with a tooltip that describes it. Models defined by a table — the shear/normal, discrete, C/Phi and anisotropic functions and the ranges of Generalized Anisotropic — show the table instead, with + Row and − Row buttons. Snowden Modified Anisotropic Linear has two buttons, Bedding Strength Function… and Rock Mass Strength Function…, and the depth-dependent undrained models an Apply cutoff box.

The equations behind each model, and how a curved envelope is used inside the methods of slices, are in Section 3.5. For Anisotropic Linear and Snowden Modified Anisotropic Linear an Anisotropic Surface: list appears below the parameters: (none - use the angle above) takes the single bedding_angle, and choosing an anisotropic surface of the model (Weak layers and anisotropic surfaces) makes the bedding follow it point by point.

When a strength cannot be used — a table whose rows are not in order, ranges with a gap, a material saved by an older version whose table now means something else — a message in red explains why below the parameters, and OK refuses the change until it is corrected. A row of a table that is not made of numbers also keeps the dialog on that material.

Hoek-Brown in 0.1.235

The envelope used by the two Hoek–Brown models gives no shear strength at zero normal stress, where the criterion's own Mohr envelope does (about 307.5 kPa with the starting constants of Generalized Hoek-Brown). The program records this as defect D171; the criterion is described in Section 3.5.

Water parameters#

The Water Parameters group decides where the pore pressure of the material comes from. It is described with the groundwater methods in Section 2.4.2; in short:

Only the fields the chosen type reads are enabled. The whole group is greyed out for the strength models that read no pore pressure — Undrained (φ=0), No Strength, Infinite Strength and the three depth-dependent undrained models — and hidden when the project uses a finite-element groundwater method.

Water

A material starts dry (none). Drawing a water table does not wet anything by itself: the materials have to point at it, which the Assign Water Surface dialog does for several at once (Assign Water Surface).

Rapid drawdown and excess pore pressure#

Two more groups appear only when the analysis that reads them is chosen on the Groundwater page of Project Settings (Section 2.1.4):

  • Rapid Drawdown Parameters: Undrained Behaviour (off) marks a material of low permeability that keeps its excess pore pressure when the water level drops. With the Effective Stress using B-bar procedure it takes a B-bar value (0 by default, 0 to 5); with the three multi-stage procedures it takes an undrained envelope, set with Define Strength… as a total-stress R envelope (intercept and angle) or a Kc=1K_c = 1Kc​=1 envelope, and summarised beside the button.
  • Excess Pore Pressure: B-bar (Skempton's Bˉ\bar BBˉ, 0 to 5) gives Δu=Bˉ Δσv\Delta u = \bar B\,\Delta\sigma_vΔu=BˉΔσv​ under the loads applied (Skempton, 1954); 0 is a free-draining material. Material weight creates excess pore pressure (off) makes the weight of this material load the materials beneath it — an embankment over a clay foundation usually has it on and a B-bar of 0.

Both analyses are described in Section 2.4.6 and Section 3.8.

2.3.2The GSI calculator#

For a material with the Generalized Hoek-Brown strength type, a GSI… button appears next to the list. It opens the Parameter Calculator, which derives the rock-mass constants from the Geological Strength Index, the intact-rock constant mim_imi​ and the disturbance factor DDD, following Hoek, Carranza-Torres & Corkum (2002):

The Calculated group shows mbm_bmb​, sss and aaa as the inputs change; Use these values writes the three into the material. The intact strength sigci is typed in the material itself. D=2D = 2D=2 makes mbm_bmb​ undefined, so it is clamped to 1.999 and the dialog says so. GSI and DDD are matters of judgement: use the descriptions rather than a remembered number.

2.3.3Assigning materials to regions#

Regions are formed by the external and material boundaries (Material boundaries and regions). To give them materials:

  1. Choose Properties → Assign Materials. A floating Assign Materials panel opens with the list of materials, each with its colour, and (None — unassign) at the top; the first material is selected.
  2. Select a material in the panel and click every region that is made of it. Each click is one undo step.
  3. Select the next material and repeat. Close the panel to return to the Select tool.

Two shortcuts do the same for one region: right-click the region and choose a material from Assign Material in its menu, which also tells you the material the region has now; or use Properties → Assign Material, the painting tool on its own, which paints with the material selected in the Assign Materials panel and therefore works only while that panel is open.

An assignment is recorded as the point clicked together with the outline the region had at that moment. If a material boundary drawn later splits the region, every piece keeps the material; if the region was already split when you clicked, only the piece you clicked takes it.

Regions without a material#

The first-material rule

A region to which no material has been assigned is made of the first material in the list of Define Materials. The analysis, the data tips and the right-click menu of the region all treat it so, and nothing warns that the region was never painted. Assign every region explicitly, or keep the most widespread material first in the list.

Version 0.1.235

The (None — unassign) entry of the Assign Materials panel does not remove an assignment yet: with it selected, a click only asks you to pick a material. To change a region, paint it with another material.

2.3.4Property tables#

Tools → Property Tables shows read-only tables of the model, with a Copy to clipboard button: the Material Properties Table (name, strength model, unit weight, saturated unit weight when used, pore-pressure type and one column per strength parameter in use), the Support Properties Table and the Hydraulic Properties Table. The material table is also on the toolbar. Hovering over a region with the data tips on shows the same properties for one material (Data tips).

2.3.5Importing properties#

File → Import Properties… copies the materials and the support types of another .ogr project into this one. After choosing the file, choose what to import: Materials, Supports or Both (the default).

  • Nothing already in the project is overwritten: an imported name that already exists — compared without regard to case — gets a number, such as Clay (2).
  • Imported materials get new identifiers, and lose their links to the other project's water surfaces and anisotropic surfaces, which do not exist here; a material that read a water surface comes in dry. Assign it to a water surface of this project (Assign Water Surface).
  • The Max Materials limit of the project is respected (Section 2.1.2).
  • Support types are imported, not placed supports.

The status bar reports how many materials and support types were imported. The materials still have to be assigned to regions.

In OGR Slip2D

Every strength model is a plug-in registered by its identifier; the list of the dialog and the parameter fields are built from the registry, so the dialog and the analysis cannot disagree on what a model takes. Values are stored in SI units and converted for display. The default of a region without a material is applied where the regions are resolved, the same place the analysis asks.
