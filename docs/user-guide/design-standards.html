2.11

Design standards

Describes OGR Slip2D 0.1.481

A design standard applies partial factors to the inputs of the analysis: strengths and unit weights are divided, the weight of the soil and the loads multiplied. The result is reported as an over-design factor, which must exceed 1, instead of a factor of safety. OGR Slip2D offers the three design approaches of Eurocode 7 as four presets (DA1 Combination 1, DA1 Combination 2, DA2 and DA3) and lets you enter your own factors. The basis of the factors is in Section 3.12.

2.11.1The Design Standard page#

The factors are set in Analysis → Project Settings → Design Standard. Apply partial factors is off in a new project: applying factors silently would change every factor of safety computed before, so it has to be a deliberate choice. With it ticked, Design standard offers No standard, Eurocode 7 — DA1 Combination 1, Eurocode 7 — DA1 Combination 2, Eurocode 7 — DA2, Eurocode 7 — DA3 and Custom standard. Choosing a standard loads its factors into the eleven boxes below; the boxes can be edited only under Custom standard. Most of them take 0.1 to 10. The two unfavourable action factors cannot be below 1.0, the favourable permanent factor goes from 0.1 to 1.0 and the favourable variable factor from 0 to 1.0, which are the bounds the analysis checks before it runs.

Two boxes below the factors decide how the action factors are laid on the slices, and they can be changed with any standard:

  • Single source (ticked by default): the soil weight of every slice takes the unfavourable permanent factor, the weight of the soil above the surface being one permanent action from one source. Unticked, a slice whose base drives the sliding takes the unfavourable factor and one whose base climbs against it the favourable one; the favourable box is read only then.
  • Anchors (unticked by default): the permanent-action factor also multiplies the effective vertical stress that the bond of a support reads, that is, the soil above it and the permanent loads. The variable loads never take a factor there.

2.11.2The presets#

The presets follow the design approaches of EN 1997-1, Annex A (CEN, 2004).

The factor for Shear strength (other models) is not a factor of EN 1997-1: the presets set it equal to the γφ′ of the same set, so that a strength envelope that cannot be split into c′ and tan φ′ is reduced as the factor of safety itself would reduce it. This is a decision of the program, and you can change it under Custom standard. The favourable variable factor of 0 is the value of EN 1997-1, Annex A: a variable load that would help the slope may not be there, so it is left out. No standard sets the eleven factors to 1.0.

2.11.3What is factored, and how#

The factors are applied to a copy of the project when an analysis starts, so your model is not modified, and switching the standard off returns the unfactored results. Every analysis computes on that copy: Compute, each sample of Compute Statistics, the Drawdown Level Sweep, Optimize Surfaces, the back analysis of support force and the factors of safety of the transient stages.

Material factors divide and action factors multiply, so each one acts in the unfavourable direction. The friction angle is factored through its tangent, as Eurocode 7 specifies: with γφ′ = 1.25, a friction angle of 30° becomes 24.79°, not 24°. Each strength model declares which of the four categories its parameters belong to; a model that declares none is left unfactored, and the analysis notes name it.

The action factors are applied slice by slice, because whether a weight or a load helps or drives depends on the surface. The sense of sliding is read from the unfactored slices; the soil weight of a slice drives when its base dips in that sense. A load is classed as a whole, by what it does on balance along the sliding, so a load that straddles the lowest point of a circle takes one factor. The factored weight is the one every term reads: the pseudo-static seismic force, the vertical stress of the strength models that use it, and the pore pressures computed from the weight (rur_uru​ and the excess pore pressure of the B-bar method). The support forces are used as entered, except for the option Anchors described above.

In OGR Slip2D

The material and seismic factors are applied to the copy, which the methods of slices and the searches read as an ordinary project. The action factors travel with the copy and are applied by the slicer, so they reach every surface of every search. The analysis notes of a factored run start with the line Design standard applied: … — the reported value is an over-design factor, not a factor of safety, and must exceed 1, followed by what was factored: the materials, the soil weight, the loads and the seismic coefficients, or that the standard is on with every factor at 1.0.

Source: ogr_slip2d/design_actions.py · OGR-Core: ogr_core/project/design_factors.py · ogr_core/project/settings.py · ogr_core/loads/actions.py

2.11.4Reading the over-design factor#

With a standard applied, the status bar, the Results dock and the Interpret window caption the critical value Over-design factor instead of factor of safety. It is computed in the same way as a factor of safety, but on the factored model, so it answers a different question: a value above 1 means that the slope meets the limit state with the partial factors chosen (Frank et al., 2004).

When the four strength factors are equal to one value γ, the resistance factor is 1 and no action, unit weight or seismic coefficient is factored, every strength is divided by γ at every normal stress, and the over-design factor is the factor of safety divided by γ, in every method:

Fd=FγF_d = \dfrac{F}{\gamma}Fd​=γF​

DA1 Combination 2 on a slope of c′–φ′ materials without external loads is that case, with γ = 1.25: a slope with a factor of safety of 1.30 has an over-design factor of 1.04.

With Apply partial factors ticked and every factor at 1.0 (the No standard preset, for example), nothing changes, but the value is still captioned over-design factor; the analysis notes say that nothing was factored. The PDF report of File → Generate Report… has a Design Standard section with the standard, what was factored and the notes of the run, and labels the value Over-design Factor.

2.11.5What is left unfactored#

Version 0.1.481

Some inputs take no partial factor, whatever the standard:

  • Water outside the soil. The weight and the thrust of ponded water and the thrust of the water in a tension crack are kept apart from the soil weight and are not factored. Pore pressures read from a water table, a piezometric line, a grid or a seepage analysis are not factored either; only those computed from the weight (rur_uru​ and the B-bar excess) follow the factored weight.
  • Supports. Their forces and capacities are used as entered. With Anchors ticked, γG reaches only the effective vertical stress that a bond reads.
